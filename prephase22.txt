16. DISTINGUISH THE USER SCENARIOS
============================================================

Do not design one destructive "Delete Maggie" action for every circumstance.

Analyse at least these scenarios separately.


SCENARIO A — CARE NO LONGER REQUIRED

Example:

Maggie has recovered or the family no longer needs active care coordination.

Desired conceptual outcome may be:

ARCHIVE CARE

Existing history remains preserved.

Maggie disappears from normal active-care switching/navigation.

Data can potentially be reviewed/exported later.


SCENARIO B — ORGANISER NO LONGER RESPONSIBLE

Example:

David is stepping away, but Marion and other Care Circle members continue
supporting Maggie.

The organiser should NOT be able to destroy Maggie's shared care space merely
because David personally is leaving.

Potential outcome:

TRANSFER RESPONSIBILITY / LEAVE CARE SPACE.


SCENARIO C — SUPPORTED PERSON ASKS TO BE REMOVED

Example:

Maggie asks that her care information no longer be managed through Lilica.

This may require a materially different workflow from archive.

Analyse:

- deletion;
- retention;
- collaborators' contributions;
- documents;
- audit history;
- former-member attribution;
- privacy implications;
- export;
- confirmation.

Do not pretend Archive satisfies an explicit deletion request.


SCENARIO D — SOLE ORGANISER

If the only organiser wants to stop caring for Maggie:

determine what should happen.

Consider:

- transfer;
- archive;
- delete;
- warning;
- outstanding Care Circle membership.


SCENARIO E — MULTIPLE ORGANISERS

Determine who should be authorised to:

- archive;
- unarchive;
- transfer responsibility;
- request/execute deletion.

Do not assume every organiser should have equal destructive authority.
