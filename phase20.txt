3. UNDERSTAND LILICA'S PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY
================================================================

Protect the product philosophy already established.

Lilica is NOT intended to become a large menu of disconnected modules.

Its primary model is:

HOME
"What should I know about the person I support right now?"

CALENDAR
"What is happening and when?"

TO DO
"What needs somebody to do something?"

PEOPLE
"Who is involved?"

ADD
"I have something new to capture."

Underlying records remain the shared source of truth.

Calendar and To Do are projections, not independent databases.

Documents should become useful through relationships/follow-up rather than
simply becoming a dead file store.

Care Circle collaboration should make coordinating care easier without
turning Lilica into an enterprise workflow product.

Any recommendation must respect this architecture unless there is an
exceptionally strong reason not to.

7. PARTICULAR AREAS I WANT EXAMINED DEEPLY
================================================================

The following are review candidates, NOT approved features.

Analyse each properly.

------------------------------------------------------------
A. RECENT ACTIVITY / CARE HISTORY
------------------------------------------------------------

Once several people help Beauty, can David answer:

"What has happened since I last looked?"

Examples:

- Marion added a GP appointment.
- David marked Water as paid.
- Sarah uploaded an insurance document.
- Jackie completed Collect prescription.
- Marion changed an appointment date.

Determine:

- whether existing audit/history infrastructure already supports this;
- what is missing;
- where activity would naturally live;
- whether it belongs under People/Care Circle;
- whether a tiny Home indication is useful or unnecessary;
- privacy/permission implications.

------------------------------------------------------------
B. SEARCH
------------------------------------------------------------

Can someone quickly find:

- an old appointment;
- a document;
- an insurance policy;
- a contact;
- a historical task;
- a bill;
- something associated with a keyword?

Determine whether the current product becomes difficult once somebody has
used it for a year.

Consider whether global search is genuinely necessary.

------------------------------------------------------------
C. MEDICATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Lilica already has medicine-related record capability.

Determine whether that is enough.

Compare against actual carer needs and Jointly.

Possible distinctions include:

- medicine information;
- dosage;
- schedule;
- repeat prescription;
- reorder;
- collection;
- administration tracking;
- adherence;
- notes;
- responsible person.

Do NOT recommend turning Lilica into a clinical medication-management system
without analysing safety/liability implications.

------------------------------------------------------------
D. CONTINGENCY / EMERGENCY INFORMATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Jointly/Carers UK may support information intended to help if the primary
carer cannot provide care.

Investigate this carefully.

Ask whether Lilica currently provides somewhere obvious for:

"If something happens to me, what does another person need to know about
Beauty?"

This could be materially important to carers.

Assess value, privacy, security and scope.

------------------------------------------------------------
E. CALENDAR INTEGRATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Investigate Jointly's calendar behaviour.

Would carers benefit from Lilica appointments appearing in:

- Apple Calendar;
- Google Calendar;
- device calendar?

Could import create duplication/confusion?

Would export/subscription be sufficient?

Assess rather than assume.

------------------------------------------------------------
F. NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------

Jointly has Notes.

Determine whether Lilica actually lacks a need here or whether:

- records;
- documents;
- task notes;
- appointment outcomes;
- general information

already cover it better.

Do not create a generic Notes feature simply to tick a box.

------------------------------------------------------------
G. MESSAGING / COMMUNICATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Jointly has messaging.

Ask whether Lilica should.

Families already use:

- WhatsApp;
- iMessage;
- SMS;
- Messenger;
- etc.

Would duplicating general chat make Lilica worse?

Alternatively, is there a genuine need for contextual comments such as:

"Spoke to GP - prescription will be ready Friday"

attached to a record/task?

Analyse the NEED, not the competitor checkbox.

------------------------------------------------------------
H. CONTACT IMPORTING
------------------------------------------------------------

Would importing from the device address book materially improve Key Contact
creation?

Assess:

- convenience;
- permission burden;
- privacy;
- cross-platform behaviour;
- implementation complexity.

------------------------------------------------------------
I. RECURRING TASKS / CHECKLISTS
------------------------------------------------------------

Determine whether Lilica's existing recurrence engine adequately serves
repeated caring responsibilities.

Consider:

- weekly shopping;
- bins;
- prescription collection;
- recurring bills;
- routine calls/check-ins;
- household tasks.

Do not duplicate existing recurrence capability.

------------------------------------------------------------
J. NOTIFICATION SOPHISTICATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Review whether the existing Phase 14 reminder engine provides enough.

Consider:

- assignment notification;
- upcoming deadline;
- overdue item;
- Care Circle activity;
- changed appointment;
- invitation;
- completed responsibility.

Separate:

REMINDER
from
COLLABORATION NOTIFICATION.

They are not necessarily the same thing.

------------------------------------------------------------
K. SUPPORTED-PERSON PROFILE INFORMATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Jointly collects substantial information about the person being cared for.

Determine which information is genuinely useful in Lilica and which would
make Lilica feel like a medical/social-care record.

Examples to assess:

- preferred name;
- date of birth;
- important health conditions;
- allergies;
- dietary requirements;
- accessibility needs;
- communication preferences;
- likes/dislikes;
- assistance needs;
- emergency information.

Remember:

Lilica's onboarding deliberately avoids forcing users through a giant dossier.

Even if additional information is valuable, it does NOT follow that it should
be collected during onboarding.

------------------------------------------------------------
L. MEMBER MANAGEMENT / FILTERING
------------------------------------------------------------

Jointly has mature circle/member concepts.

Compare against Lilica's current Care Circle.

Assess whether managing a larger family/helper group exposes gaps around:

- member list;
- role;
- relationship;
- permissions;
- filtering;
- former members;
- invitations;
- activity;
- responsibilities.

------------------------------------------------------------
M. OFFLINE
------------------------------------------------------------

Jointly has historically emphasised practical carer access.

Lilica already has substantial offline architecture.

Determine whether this is:

- parity;
- advantage;
- or whether important workflows remain online-only.

Do not rebuild the sync system.

------------------------------------------------------------
N. EXPORT / CONTINUITY
------------------------------------------------------------

Compare Jointly's export/contingency behaviour with Lilica's current export.

Ask whether Lilica's export is technically complete but not useful enough to
a human carer.

Could there eventually be a useful human-readable:

- care summary;
- emergency summary;
- handover summary;

distinct from raw data export?

Analyse only.
