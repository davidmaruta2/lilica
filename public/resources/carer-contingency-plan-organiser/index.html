Carer contingency plan organiser

Prepare backup contacts and practical first steps if the usual carer is unavailable. It works on paper and does not require Lilica or any other app.

Last reviewed: 3 November 2026Review owner: Luxford Interactive Ltd
All resources
Important: This is a general organisation aid, not medical, legal or financial advice. It does not replace a care plan, medicines list, professional assessment or instructions from a health or care professional. Lilica is not an emergency service. For a life-threatening emergency call 999.[1] If someone needs medical help now and it is not life-threatening, use the appropriate urgent-care service for your UK nation; in England, Scotland and Wales this may include NHS 111. Northern Ireland has different urgent-care arrangements.[2]

A contingency plan explains the first practical steps if you, the usual unpaid carer, cannot provide support. It may be needed because you are ill, delayed, admitted to hospital or dealing with another urgent situation.

The plan does not have to solve everything. Its job is to help an agreed backup person find current information, contact the right services and cover the most important practical tasks until a longer arrangement is made.

Make the plan with the person you support wherever possible. Ask each backup person before naming them. Give people access only to the parts they need.

Keep two versions

Quick contact card: enough information to alert the first agreed person and locate the full plan. Carry it only if you are comfortable doing so.

Full plan: practical detail for authorised backups. Store it securely. Do not put passwords, PINs, full card details, authentication codes, safe combinations or key-safe codes in a widely shared copy.

Carers UK includes a contingency-planning tool called MyBackUp within its information on arranging care and support.[3] Your council, local carers service or care provider may also have a local scheme or preferred template. This organiser can help you prepare questions, but it does not enrol anyone in a response service.

The NHS says that in England repeat prescriptions may be ordered through the NHS App/website, another service used by the GP surgery, or by contacting the surgery.[4] Record the route that actually applies. NHS 111 online can request an emergency supply of regularly prescribed medicine in some circumstances in England; this is not the same as issuing a prescription, and nation arrangements differ.[2]

Do not change or stop medicines based on this organiser.

7. Home access and practical information

  • Backup helper knows how to request authorised access.
  • Security information is held separately and shared only when needed.
  • Pets are accounted for.
  • Essential equipment/provider contacts are listed.
  • Utility or property issues have an agreed contact.
  • The backup knows how to leave the home secure.

In England, an adult unpaid carer can ask the local council for a free carer's assessment, which considers how caring affects their life. A carer's assessment is separate from the needs assessment for the person receiving care, although the two can be requested together.[5] Ask during an assessment what contingency support exists locally. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have different systems and terminology, so check the route in your nation.

9. Longer than 24 hours

If the absence continues, the first backup may need professional or local-authority help rather than taking on an open-ended role.

  • Who decides that the short-term plan is no longer enough?
  • Which council/service/provider should be contacted?
  • What existing assessment or care plan should be reviewed?
  • Who updates the wider family or helper group?
  • Who can care for pets, the home or other dependants?
  • What work, school or travel commitments affect backups?

11. Test and review

A plan may be outdated or a named person may no longer be available. Checking the plan improves its usefulness but cannot guarantee a response.

  • Ask each named person to confirm their role.
  • Check phone numbers and service opening/response arrangements.
  • Confirm the person receiving support still agrees.
  • Check the medicines-list and formal-plan locations.
  • Confirm access arrangements without circulating codes.
  • Walk through a simple scenario with the first backup.
  • Remove people who no longer need access.
  • Set a review date and review after any major change.

If you need to make a plan today

Do these six things first:

  1. Ask one person whether they can be your first backup.
  2. Write a second contact and one local/service contact.
  3. List the essential first-day tasks.
  4. Record where the current medicines list and care plan are held.
  5. Agree how an authorised backup gets access without putting codes in the plan.
  6. Give the quick card and full plan only to the people who need them.
