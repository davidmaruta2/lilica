Getting a carer's assessment: what to prepare (England)

A carer's assessment is about you, not the person you care for. Preparing a few notes beforehand helps you say what is really happening.

Last reviewed: 5 November 2026Review owner: Luxford Interactive Ltd

If you look after a relative, partner or friend who could not manage without your help, you may be able to ask your local council in England for a carer's assessment.[1] It looks at how caring affects your life and whether you could get support. This guide covers England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own processes, and Carers UK explains the differences.[2]

Important: This is general information, not legal or financial advice. What support you can get depends on your circumstances and your council. Check the official pages before relying on anything here.

What the assessment is for

The assessment is about your wellbeing and your ability to keep caring. It can cover your health, work, study, family life, free time and sleep. It does not judge how well you care.

Before the assessment: make a few notes

People often play down how much they do. Spending twenty minutes writing things down helps you describe a typical week honestly.

  • What you do: personal care, meals, medicines reminders, lifts, shopping, housework, paperwork, phone calls, emotional support.
  • How long it takes: an approximate number of hours in a normal week, including nights.
  • The impact on you: sleep, health, work hours, social life, money, stress.
  • What is hard: the tasks or times of day that feel too much.
  • What would help: a regular break, help with transport, equipment, a sitting service, someone to talk to.
  • What happens if you cannot care: who else could step in, and what is the plan?

On the day

  • Be honest about bad days, not just the good ones.
  • You can ask for the assessment to be done separately from the person you care for.
  • You can have a friend or relative with you.
  • Ask what happens next, and by when.

Afterwards

Keep a copy of anything written down. If you are told you are eligible for support, ask what is being offered and how to review it later. If you disagree with the outcome, ask the council how to challenge it. A carers organisation near you may be able to help.

Other support to check

Carer's Allowance is a separate benefit with its own rules on caring hours and earnings.[3] It is worth checking even if you are not sure you qualify.
